## What physical size of the semiconductor limits the line width of gamma lines?

Germanium: indirect semiconductor
- Gamma Lines:  Lines detected with the Ge- Detector
- Line width: The widthness of a monochromatic emitter should be infinitely thin. Line width then describes the energy resolution of the semiconductor

Line width depends on the free charge carriers of the semiconductor ($n_e$) and of course the ionization energy/ band structure and fluctuates statistically

- Indirect semiconductor such as germanium has a lower ionization energy which helps in freeing more electrons 
- Also Germanium has a relatively high atmoic number ($Z = 32$) which helps as photoelectric $\sigma$ scales with $Z^n$ .

effects that broaden the line further: noise (thermal noise for example)

## How can the full energy probability of germanium detectors be determinend using a point source at large distances?

Translated:
Full energy probability means: The Probability, that the total energy of the incoming photon is actually being measured.

How to measure:

1. Look at the full energy peaks (where the energies that are being emitted by Eu for example are measured).  Integrate over them to determine their counts

2. Apply the fomula $\epsilon(E) = \frac{N}{A(t)\cdot t \cdot p_\gamma \cdot \Omega/4\pi}$ with $p_\gamma$ being the branching ratio, $A(t)$ being the activity and $t$ being the time of measurement. 
 this basically is just the number of detected full-energy peaks of all the photons that are expected to hit the detector in a given amount of time with this energy $E$.
